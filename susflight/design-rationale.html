Introduction

The Sustainable Flight Project aims to explore solutions for sustainable flight, especially regarding how we can make more informed and conscious travel decisions that minimally impact the environment. We decided to use sustainable flight because as Singaporeans, we automatically default to taking a flight out of the island when going anywhere, even places like Bali or KL.

Estimations

Estimated values:

  • Distance: Great-circle distance from Singapore Changi Airport
  • Aircraft Type: A320 if 3000km or lower, B789 if 3000-9000km, A359 if over 9000km
  • Fuel: OpenAP cruise fuel flow with 80% maximum takeoff weight with fixed amounts for takeoff and landing (800 kg for narrow-bodies, 2000 kg for wide-bodies)
  • CO2 Emissions: 3.16 kg CO2 per kg of fuel burned
  • Passenger Share: 90% for A320, 80% for B789 and A359
  • Passenger Load Factor: 80% for all aircraft types
    • PYTHON
    We imported pathlib, math, csv, openAP, and json. Pathlib is for accessing files and writes to files (as used when writing the routes), math is used to import the sin, cos, arctan, radians, and square root functions of the math module (for haversine), openap is used to calculate fuel flow and maximum takeoff weight (properties of plane), and json is for notating objects. CSV is used as a way to format data that we had.
    Haversine is a formula used to calculate the distance from two locations based on their latitudes and longitudes. The parameters only include latitude and longitude, as Singapore is always a constant location (from); therefore, the longitude and latitude of the destination are always those of Singapore.
    For aircraft type, after finding the distance with haversine, we filter the aircraft solely based on the distance traveled. Within the range of 0 - 3000 kilometers, the aircraft is an A320. Between 3000 and 9000 kilometers, it’s a B789, and anything further is an A359. (These are just common aircraft for their respective distances)
    A dictionary is used to define the aircraft's “attributes” (e.g., number of seats, fuel, ratio of people to total space (the leftover amount is assumed to be the payload), and cruising speed).
    Trip takes the aircraft type and distance as parameters to calculate the total fuel consumption. The maximum takeoff weight (MTOW) is assumed to be 80%, the speed of the plane is then converted from knots to kmph, and the equation of the fuel consumption in kg is given by the equation float(fuel_flow) * hours * 3600 + fuels[aircraft].
    For co2perguy (base amount), the load factor (force of takeoff) is assumed to be 0.8 for all aircraft. Given the aircraft and distance, the function calls trip to calculate the total emissions. For every kg of fuel burnt, it releases 3.16 kgs of CO2. To get the individual amount, we divided the total emissions by the seating capacity denoted by the dictionary and the aircraft type, multiplied by the load factor to estimate the occupied seats. | -> In the app.js file, cabin class is accounted for
    A loop is used to obtain the latitude and longitude of the airport.
    Routes is a summary of all the data, formatted with the emissions per passenger, flight, city, country, IATA code, airport, latitude, longitude, and distance from Singapore. Each element of the routes list is one location with all the aforementioned data. It is then exported using PathLib into the raw folder defined from the start.
